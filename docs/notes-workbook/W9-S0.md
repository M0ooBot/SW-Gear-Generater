# W9 S0: the user's sprocket on a gear Update puts back, the grounded tie, and What's Wrong

The questions are ISSUE-1-PLAN §4's S0 and P0, as revised after the second review (S5):

- which route K2 takes when Update puts a train back to its built frames while a user's component is locked to one
  of its gears;
- what the read before a press says when a user mate ties a gear to something fixed;
- whether a failing rebuild opens What's Wrong.

The plan named two routes: (a) the put carries the component and nothing of the user's is written; (b) the user's
mates on the train are suppressed around the put and turned back on. The first run showed that neither keeps a
locked component on its gear. The coordinator then ruled a third route, (c), which this note also measures: the app
puts the user's locked component along with its gear.

## P0: What's Wrong

"Show errors every rebuild" (`swUserPreferenceToggle_e` 77) was on as found, on both versions, and stayed on. The
setup was a scratch strip gear with a user keyway: a Ø3 cut through the bore's edge, made by the session's own sketch
and cut calls. Bore Diameter went 10 → 14, which left the keyway inside the bore. On both versions:

- the keyway went into error, `GetErrorCode2` **71** on 2026 and **51** on 2024 (`ICE`, warning flag False);
- the part's rebuild returned **False**;
- `GetWhatsWrongCount` read **1**;
- **no window** came up in the 8 s after.

So a **part's** rebuild through the API (`s.rebuild()`, which for a part is `ForceRebuild3(False)`) that fails on a
user feature does not open What's Wrong, on a visible, active part opened by the app's route, with the toggle on. This
also answers M3 in part: a part's rebuild with only a user feature in error returns False. An **assembly's** rebuild
(Update's `ForceRebuild3(True)`) with only a user mate or feature in error was not run: whether it opens What's Wrong
or reports failure stays the plan's M3, in W9a's demonstration. The error codes (71, 51) are not read for meaning, as
`findings.MATE_ERROR_2024`'s comment rules.

## Route (c): the user's locked components put with their gears

The coordinator's ruling. It follows `swbuild._put_train`, which puts both bodies of the app's own locked pairs:

- **What is put.** Every user component rigid to an app gear through the user's locks, directly or through another
  user component, is put with the gear's rigid motion: U_target = G_target · inv(G_before) · U_before.
- **When.** In the mode shown, after the app's locks, gear mates and couplings on there are suppressed and the
  assembly rebuilt. The app's bodies go first (carriers first), then the user's components, each read back by
  `place_component`. Then a rebuild and the mates back on (`put_c_job`).
- **(c1)** leaves the user's locks on.
- **(c2)** suppresses them in the mode around the puts and turns them back on, each flag read back in every
  configuration.

| Case | What is locked | (c1) on 2026 | (c2) on 2026 | (c1) on 2024 | (c2) on 2024 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Sprocket-1 to Sun | clean, worst 2.9e-15 | clean, 2.5e-15 | clean, 3.6e-15 | clean, 3.1e-15 |
| B | Sprocket-2 to RangeSlv (slides by mode) | clean, 7.1e-15 | clean, 7.1e-15 | clean, 7.1e-15 | clean, 7.1e-15 |
| E | A, plus Sprocket-3 locked to Sprocket-1 (a chain) | clean, 4.1e-15 | clean, 3.5e-15 | clean, 5.1e-15 | clean, 4.4e-15 |

The worst figure is the largest gap, over every read of the case, between a locked sprocket's pose in its gear's

- every body put, the user's included, read at its target to 6.6e-10 or better with the mates back on;

- **H's put does not move L wrongly.** In A, B and E, L shown after H's put read Sun at its built turn, with every

## The tie (C and C2)

- **C** (Sprocket-1 locked to Sun, its Top plane coincident with the fixed Housing-1's): every body of Sun's train
  read **3**, fully defined, before any put. That held unshown in H, shown in H and shown in L, on both runs on 2026.
  Every body is Sun, the three planet copies, the carrier, HighDog, LowDog and RangeSlv. Every case without the tie
  read 2 for every mated body. No read gave 4, but SolidWorks itself said the assembly was over defined during the
  drive (next item), so a tie whose geometry conflicts could read 4: the app's test is "not
  `COMPONENT_UNDER_DEFINED`", as `swbuild._free_to_turn` makes it, never "equal to 3".
- **N was not read before a put.** It was read only in the last read, after the puts, and there the tied set differs:
  RangeSlv, not coupled in N, read 2 (run 2 line 945). So the bodies a tie holds follow the mode's couplings even
  when every user flag is the same in every mode.
- **The drive against the tie** turned nothing, and the drive job returned no error (`errors []`), but SolidWorks
  raised its balloon tip "The assembly is over defined. Click here to view mate errors." during it, on both versions
  (run 2 line 917, before the drive's step line at 920; run 3 line 1843, before 1848). The reads after it found no
  mate in error and status 3. So the drive's own error read did not see SolidWorks' over-definition; the likeliest
  reading is that the drive's angle mate, deleted before the read, is what over-defined the assembly.
- **In run 1, a put of Sun alone +37° against the tie was taken**, with no mate in error: the user's lock re-took
  that offset. The tie does not stop a put, so refusing before the press is the app's only defence.
- **C2** (the tie suppressed in H and N, on in L): the unshown read with H active said free, and so did H shown. L
  shown said tied. **The showing mode's read does not decide** when a user mate's flag differs by mode; that mode

puts clean. C2 on 2024 (run 4): the unshown read with H active and H shown read the train free, every mated body 2

## When a turn reaches another mode

Case 0 of run 2 showed L after H's drive and before H's put: Sun stood at +37° there (RangeSlv at H's 47.59°), so the
drive's turn reached L. Then, after H's put, L shown again still read +37°: H's put did not carry to L.

In every case where L was first shown only after H's put, L read at its built frames. This was so in run 1 and in
run 2's cases A to E. So whether L holds H's turn depends on whether L was shown while the train stood turned. A mode
shown after the turn and before the put keeps the turn. A mode first shown after the put reads built. The run does not
explain this; it records it. 2024 read the same in case 0 (L shown after H's drive: Sun at +37°). In A to E,
C and D, L shown after H's put kept every locked sprocket at its pose of the setup.

Update puts every mode it shows (ruling 9.18), so this matters only for what a mode looks like between presses.
