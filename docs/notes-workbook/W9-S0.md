# W9 S0: the user's sprocket on a gear Update puts back, the grounded tie, and What's Wrong

The questions are ISSUE-1-PLAN §4's S0 and P0, as revised after the second review (S5):

- which route K2 takes when Update puts a train back to its built frames while a user's component is locked to one
  of its gears;
- what the read before a press says when a user mate ties a gear to something fixed;
- whether a failing rebuild opens What's Wrong.

The plan named two routes: (a) the put carries the component and nothing of the user's is written; (b) the user's
mates on the train are suppressed around the put and turned back on. The first run showed that neither keeps a
locked component on its gear. The coordinator then ruled a third route, (c), which this note also measures: the app
puts the user's locked component along with its gear.

**The evidence**, all from `probe/w9_user_work_check.py`. Every number below is from these runs' logs and JSONs in
`probe/results/` (2024's in `sw2024/`). Nothing goes into `findings.py`; the proposed findings are at the end.

## P0: What's Wrong

"Show errors every rebuild" (`swUserPreferenceToggle_e` 77) was on as found, on both versions, and stayed on. The
setup was a scratch strip gear with a user keyway: a Ø3 cut through the bore's edge, made by the session's own sketch
and cut calls. Bore Diameter went 10 → 14, which left the keyway inside the bore. On both versions:

- the keyway went into error, `GetErrorCode2` **71** on 2026 and **51** on 2024 (`ICE`, warning flag False);
- the part's rebuild returned **False**;
- `GetWhatsWrongCount` read **1**;
- **no window** came up in the 8 s after.

So a rebuild through the API that fails on a user feature does not open What's Wrong. This also answers M3 in part:
a part's rebuild with only a user feature in error returns False.

The drive carried the locked sprocket exactly. Then, in both routes, the put moved the gear back and left the

- **Route (a)** (lock left on): the lock took the new offset with code 0.

This is `findings.LOCK_RETAKES_AN_OFFSET`, which holds for a lock left on through a `Transform2` put of one of its

code 0. Its pose in Sun's frame was off by 0.60 after H's put and 0.96 after L's (74°).

## Route (c): the user's locked components put with their gears

The coordinator's ruling. It follows `swbuild._put_train`, which puts both bodies of the app's own locked pairs:

- **What is put.** Every user component rigid to an app gear through the user's locks, directly or through another
  user component, is put with the gear's rigid motion: U_target = G_target · inv(G_before) · U_before.
- **When.** In the mode shown, after the app's locks, gear mates and couplings on there are suppressed and the
  assembly rebuilt. The app's bodies go first (carriers first), then the user's components, each read back by
  `place_component`. Then a rebuild and the mates back on (`put_c_job`).
- **(c1)** leaves the user's locks on.
- **(c2)** suppresses them in the mode around the puts and turns them back on, each flag read back in every
  configuration.

| Case | What is locked | (c1) on 2026 | (c2) on 2026 | (c1) on 2024 | (c2) on 2024 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Sprocket-1 to Sun | clean, worst 2.9e-15 | clean, 2.5e-15 | clean, 3.6e-15 | clean, 3.1e-15 |
| B | Sprocket-2 to RangeSlv (slides by mode) | clean, 7.1e-15 | clean, 7.1e-15 | clean, 2.6e-15 | clean, 2.6e-15 |
| E | A, plus Sprocket-3 locked to Sprocket-1 (a chain) | clean, 4.1e-15 | clean, 3.5e-15 | clean, 5.1e-15 | clean, 4.4e-15 |

The worst figure is the largest gap, over every read of the case, between a locked sprocket's pose in its gear's
frame and the pose as set up. "Clean" means every one of the case's four puts (two in H, two in L) met all of these:

- every body put, the user's included, read at its target to 6.6e-10 or better with the mates back on;
- each locked sprocket stood at its pose of the setup;
- no mate read an error code;
- under (c2), the user's locks read back on in H, N and L;
- no read marked a document.

Beyond the puts:

- **No offset is re-taken.** After the first put, a further rebuild left each locked sprocket at its pose. The
  second drive carried it exactly, to 2e-16 against before the drive. The second put brought it home again. The 74°
  of route (a) is gone.
- **Every mode, shown in turn after the last put**, had every app body at its built frame (6.6e-10) and every locked
  sprocket at its pose of the setup, in H, N and L.
- **H's put does not move L wrongly.** In A, B and E, L shown after H's put read Sun at its built turn, with every
  locked sprocket at its pose of the setup. The check was made before L's drive.
- **C2 under (c1)**: H's put took Sprocket-1 back with Sun. So when L was shown, with the tie on there, Sun stood at
  its built turn. In run 1's route (a), L had stood at −37° with no error.
- **The tie (C)** still reads as a tie under (c1): the drive turned nothing, and the puts, which had nothing to move,
  were clean.

## D: a sprocket on the sliding RangeSlv, no lock

Sprocket-2 sat on Output Axis with its Front plane coincident with RangeSlv's, and no lock. It was put by the app's
route with nothing of the user's moved. As set up it stood on RangeSlv's plane in every mode (z 0 mm from RangeSlv's,
which slides by ±5 mm between modes).

After the drives and puts in H and L, and in the last read with each mode shown in turn, it stayed on RangeSlv's
plane: H, N and L each read 0 to 1e-9 mm. No mate read an error code. RangeSlv and Sprocket-2 read
`GetConstrainedStatus` 2 throughout, so nothing was over-defined. The drive turned RangeSlv and not the sprocket,
since the plane mate does not hold a turn. On 2024 D read the same: 0 mm from RangeSlv's plane in every put and in
H, N and L in the last read, and no mate in error.

## The tie (C and C2)

- **C** (Sprocket-1 locked to Sun, its Top plane coincident with the fixed Housing-1's): every body of Sun's train
  read **3**, fully defined, before any put. That held unshown in H, shown in H and shown in L, on both runs on 2026.
  Every body is Sun, the three planet copies, the carrier, HighDog, LowDog and RangeSlv. Every case without the tie
  read 2 for every mated body.
- **The drive against the tie** turned nothing, and the drive job saw no error.
- **In run 1, a put of Sun alone +37° against the tie was taken**, with no mate in error: the user's lock re-took
  that offset. The tie does not stop a put, so refusing before the press is the app's only defence.
- **C2** (the tie suppressed in H and N, on in L): the unshown read with H active said free, and so did H shown. L
  shown said tied. **The showing mode's read does not decide** when a user mate's flag differs by mode; that mode
  must be read when shown, as K2 says.

On 2024, C read the same: every body of the train 3 unshown and shown in H and L, the drive turning nothing, and both
puts clean. C2 was not read on 2024 (see the head of this note).

## When a turn reaches another mode

Case 0 of run 2 showed L after H's drive and before H's put: Sun stood at +37° there (RangeSlv at H's 47.59°), so the
drive's turn reached L. Then, after H's put, L shown again still read +37°: H's put did not carry to L.

In every case where L was first shown only after H's put, L read at its built frames. This was so in run 1 and in
run 2's cases A to E. So whether L holds H's turn depends on whether L was shown while the train stood turned. A mode
shown after the turn and before the put keeps the turn. A mode first shown after the put reads built. The run does not
explain this; it records it. 2024 read the same in case 0 (L shown after H's drive: Sun at +37°). In A to E,
C and D, L shown after H's put kept every locked sprocket at its pose of the setup.

Update puts every mode it shows (ruling 9.18), so this matters only for what a mode looks like between presses.

## Proposed findings (for the review; not in `findings.py`)

- **`USER_MATE_PUT_ROUTE`**: route **(c1)** on 2026 and on 2024: put each user component rigid to a put gear through
  the user's locks with that gear, the user's locks left on. It writes nothing of the user's but the components'
  frames, and is clean in A, B and E.
  - (c2) is as clean, but suppresses and restores the user's locks, which writes more of the user's.
  - Routes (a) and (b) are ruled out, because the lock re-takes the offset silently.
  - Evidence: run 2's `propose each route's cases` and `propose USER_MATE_PUT_ROUTE` lines, and each case's
    `the second put back to the built frames in L: … clean True` line.
  - A fact for the record: `USER_LOCK_RETAKES_PUT_OFFSET = True` on 2026 and on 2024, from run 1's A-a and A-b
    tables.
- **`USER_TIE_READ`**: `GetConstrainedStatus` **3** on every body of the tied train (never 4 here), read in the mode
  shown, on 2026 and on 2024 (C). The read in the showing mode decides every mode only when every user mate on the
  train has one flag in every mode; C2's L had to be shown (2026; C2 is owed on 2024). Evidence: the `propose
  USER_TIE_READ` lines, and `ok S0 C2: in H the train reads free` / `in L the train reads not free`.
- **`WHATS_WRONG_OPENS_ON_API_REBUILD`**: **False** on 2026 and on 2024. Evidence: each run's P0 lines: the toggle
  on, the keyway's code 71 (2026) / 51 (2024), rebuild False, `GetWhatsWrongCount` 1, no new window.
- **D**: `USER_PLANE_MATE_ON_SLIDER_FOLLOWS = True` (2026 and 2024). A user component mated to a sliding app gear
  by a plane mate and no lock follows it in every mode, with nothing of the user's moved. Evidence: run 2's `ok S0 D:
  in the last read Sprocket-2 stays on RangeSlv's Front plane in every mode`.

## Not done, and why

- **The two-train user mate** (a lock from Sun to RangeSlv) is not in S0's cases. The inventory refuses it before
  any read.
- **Which user components are tied comes from the case's own list of the user's locks**, not from reading each
  lock's entities in SolidWorks (`MateEntity(i).ReferenceComponent`, M1). W9a's worker must find the chain by that
  read.
- **A moving put against the tie** was measured in run 1 only, under route (a). Under (c) the tie is refused before
  the press, so it was not repeated.
